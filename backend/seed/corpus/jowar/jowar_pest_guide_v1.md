# Jowar Dead Heart Complex — Shoot Fly & Stem Borer

## Symptoms
Shoot fly: the central shoot of a young plant wilts and dries out
completely, forming what's called a "dead heart" — the growing point
itself is destroyed, so a gentle pull on the central leaf whorl slides
it out easily, often with a rotten smell and no resistance.

Stem borer: also produces dead heart on younger plants, but
additionally shows small round "shot holes" punched through the
whorl leaves in a characteristic row pattern, and internal stem
tunnelling; on older plants past the dead-heart-susceptible stage,
damage instead shows as weakened stems that can lodge (fall over) in
wind.

## Affected Parts
The central growing shoot and stem, for both. Stem borer additionally
damages leaf whorls (shot holes) and, on older plants, the standing
stem structure itself (lodging risk).

## Similar Pests
Both produce dead heart on young plants — identical visible sign,
different insect, and critically, a different management window.
Shot holes in the whorl leaves in a neat row pattern are specific to
stem borer and are not produced by shoot fly. The single most
reliable field-independent discriminator is plant age: shoot fly
causes dead heart on plants roughly 30 days after sowing or younger;
stem borer's dead-heart damage typically appears after that window,
on somewhat older plants, alongside the shot-hole sign.

## Causes
Shoot fly (Atherigona soccata) is a small fly whose maggot bores into
the central shoot of a young seedling and kills the growing point from
inside. Stem borer (Chilo partellus) is a moth whose larva similarly
bores into the central shoot on young plants but continues tunnelling
through the stem as the plant ages, which is why its damage profile
changes from dead heart (young plant) to stem weakening and lodging
(older plant).

## Prevention
Sow within the recommended window for the region and avoid staggered
or delayed sowing near an already-emerged jowar field, since shoot fly
populations build on earlier-sown crops and then move to later-sown
ones. Use shoot-fly-tolerant varieties where available, since variety
choice is one of the more effective levers against this particular
pest.

## Cultural Management
Higher seed rate with later thinning compensates for expected dead
heart loss without needing to resow. Remove and destroy dead hearts as
soon as spotted — the maggot or larva is often still inside and this
interrupts its cycle before it can complete development. Deep summer
ploughing exposes overwintering pupae of both pests to predators and
heat.

## Biological Management
Release Trichogramma chilonis egg parasitoid at first pest sighting
for stem borer, repeated at 7–10 day intervals. Conserve natural
predators of shoot fly maggots by avoiding early, precautionary
broad-spectrum sprays, which do little against an already-internal
maggot but do kill the natural enemies that would otherwise help
control the next generation.

## Chemical Management
Seed treatment with Thiamethoxam 30% FS at 6 ml/kg seed before sowing
is the primary chemical lever against shoot fly, since foliar sprays
after dead heart has formed cannot rescue an already-destroyed growing
point — treatment has to happen before or at emergence. For stem
borer, Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC at 0.3 ml/L water, PHI 21 days,
re-entry 24 hours, applied at first sign of shot holes in the whorl,
before larvae bore deeper into the stem where sprays are far less
effective.

## Monitoring
Inspect the crop every 3–4 days from emergence through roughly 30 days
after sowing, checking for wilted central shoots. Note plant age
(days after sowing) at the time of the field visit — this single fact
often resolves which of the two pests is responsible without needing
any other visual check. After 30 days, shift monitoring toward
shot-hole rows in whorl leaves and any stem weakening or early lodging
signs.

## When To Contact Expert
Dead heart incidence crossing roughly 10% of plants despite seed
treatment; any stem lodging appearing before harvest, since this
signals stem borer damage has progressed further than typical
chemical intervention windows allow for.
