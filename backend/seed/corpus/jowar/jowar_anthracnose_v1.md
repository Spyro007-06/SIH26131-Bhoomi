# Jowar Anthracnose

## Symptoms
Reddish-brown lesions on leaves that enlarge and merge as the disease
progresses, eventually covering large sections of the leaf surface;
small dark fruiting bodies (acervuli) visible within older lesions;
severe infections cause extensive leaf drying and premature death of
affected leaves; in advanced cases the disease can also affect the
stalk and panicle, reducing grain fill.

## Early Symptoms
Small reddish flecks or spots on lower leaves, easy to miss before
they enlarge into the characteristic reddish-brown lesions.

## Advanced Symptoms
Large coalesced reddish-brown lesions covering much of the leaf;
extensive leaf drying and premature leaf death; stalk lesions in
severe cases, weakening the plant structurally; reduced grain fill
where the panicle is affected.

## Affected Parts
Leaves primarily; stalk and panicle in severe, advanced infections.

## Similar Diseases
Downy mildew also produces discoloured streaking on leaves, but downy
mildew's chlorotic (yellow-white) streaks with a white-grey downy
fungal growth on the underside are visually distinct from anthracnose's
reddish-brown lesions with dark fruiting bodies — the colour and the
presence of fuzzy downy growth versus dark dot-like fruiting bodies is
the reliable separator between the two.

## Causes
A fungal disease (Colletotrichum graminicola), favoured by warm, humid
weather with frequent rain, and spread by rain-splash and wind within
a standing crop. The fungus also survives on crop residue between
seasons, making residue management a meaningful prevention lever.

## Prevention
Use anthracnose-tolerant varieties where available for the region.
Avoid dense plant spacing that traps humidity within the canopy. Crop
rotation with a non-host crop for at least one season reduces
carryover inoculum in field residue.

## Cultural Management
Remove and destroy infected crop residue after harvest rather than
leaving it in the field, since the fungus survives on residue over
the off-season. Avoid overhead irrigation, particularly during humid
spells when infection risk is highest. Maintain adequate row spacing
for airflow through the canopy.

## Biological Management
Seed treatment with Trichoderma viride (4 g/kg seed) before sowing
suppresses seed-borne and early-season inoculum. Foliar application of
Pseudomonas fluorescens-based biocontrol products during early
vegetative stage can reduce disease pressure where chemical use is
being minimised.

## Chemical Management
Mancozeb 75% WP at 2.5 g/L water, PHI 15 days, re-entry 24 hours,
applied at first sign of reddish-brown leaf lesions and repeated at
10–14 day intervals if humid weather continues. Where stalk or panicle
involvement is confirmed, a systemic fungicide such as Propiconazole
25% EC at 1 ml/L water, PHI 20 days, re-entry 24 hours, gives better
protection of developing grain than a contact fungicide alone.

## Monitoring
Inspect lower and mid-canopy leaves weekly from early vegetative
stage, especially after rain — early reddish flecks are easiest to
catch on lower leaves before they climb and merge. During grain
development, check the panicle directly for early lesions rather than
waiting for visible grain damage.

## When To Contact Expert
Lesions spreading onto the stalk or panicle, since this directly
threatens grain yield and quality; disease progressing rapidly despite
an initial fungicide round, which can indicate resistance or a
particularly favourable weather run worth flagging regionally.
