# Jowar Shoot Bug

## Symptoms
Sap-sucking feeding on leaves and the shoot causes yellowing and
progressive drying of leaves, starting from the lower canopy;
affected plants become visibly weak and stunted compared to
neighbouring healthy plants; unlike shoot fly and stem borer, there is
no dead heart — the central growing point is not directly destroyed,
the damage is a general sap-drain effect across the plant.

## Affected Parts
Leaves and the tender shoot region, where nymphs and adults cluster to
feed on plant sap.

## Similar Pests
Shoot bug's yellowing and stunting can superficially resemble early
nutrient stress or drought, but shoot bug damage is typically patchy
within a field (following where insect populations have clustered)
rather than uniform across the field the way drought stress usually
is. Unlike shoot fly or stem borer, shoot bug never produces a dead
heart — the growing point remains intact even in a heavily infested
plant, which is the clearest way to rule out the dead-heart complex.

## Causes
A sap-sucking bug (Peregrinus maidis-related complex, locally referred
to as jowar shoot bug) that feeds directly on plant sap from leaves
and the tender shoot, weakening the plant without boring into tissue
the way shoot fly and stem borer do.

## Prevention
Avoid staggered or delayed sowing near already-emerged jowar fields,
since shoot bug populations, like shoot fly, tend to move from
earlier-sown crops to later-sown ones as the earlier crop matures past
the susceptible stage. Maintain field-border sanitation to reduce
grassy weed hosts the bug can also breed on.

## Cultural Management
Avoid water stress during early growth stages, since drought-stressed
plants show compounded yellowing that makes shoot bug damage harder to
read and the plants themselves more vulnerable to real yield loss from
the added sap-drain. Remove and destroy heavily infested plants at
field edges early, before populations spread inward.

## Biological Management
Conserve natural predators (spiders, predatory bugs, and lady
beetles) by avoiding unnecessary precautionary sprays early in the
season. Neem seed kernel extract (5%) as a foliar spray suppresses
shoot bug populations with lower impact on natural enemies than a
synthetic insecticide.

## Chemical Management
Imidacloprid 17.8% SL at 0.3 ml/L water, PHI 15 days, re-entry 24
hours, applied once yellowing and stunting are confirmed as
shoot-bug-driven (not drought or nutrient stress) and nymph/adult
counts cross threshold on scouted leaves. Avoid routine calendar
spraying against this pest — sap-sucker populations fluctuate quickly
with weather, and an unnecessary spray both wastes cost and removes
natural enemies that would otherwise suppress the next generation.

## Monitoring
Walk the field weekly from early vegetative stage, checking leaves and
the tender shoot region for nymph/adult clusters, alongside general
yellowing and stunting. Confirm the growing point is intact (no dead
heart) before attributing yellowing to shoot bug rather than the
dead-heart complex, since the management response differs completely.

## When To Contact Expert
Yellowing and stunting spreading rapidly across a field despite
confirming no dead heart and no obvious drought stress; any case where
shoot bug and drought symptoms are hard to separate in the field,
since the correct response (irrigation vs. insecticide) depends on
telling them apart correctly.
