# Jowar Panicle & Grain Diseases — Grain Mold & Smut

## Symptoms
Grain mold: discoloured, moldy-looking grains within the panicle,
ranging from grey to black or pink fungal growth depending on the
species involved; affected grains show poor quality, reduced weight,
and are often unfit for sale or seed use even when the plant otherwise
looks healthy.

Smut: black, powdery spore masses replace individual grains or entire
sections of the panicle; affected structures are swollen and enclosed
in a membrane before rupturing to release the black spore powder,
which is the most recognisable field sign.

## Affected Parts
The panicle and developing grain for both — neither affects leaves or
stems in any meaningful way, which itself is a useful field clue when
distinguishing these two from jowar's leaf diseases (anthracnose,
downy mildew).

## Similar Diseases
Both affect the panicle and grain rather than the leaves, so a photo
focused on discoloured grain could suggest either. The discriminator:
smut replaces grain with a solid black powdery mass enclosed in a
membrane that ruptures to release spores — a completely different
grain replaced by fungal tissue. Grain mold instead shows discoloured
but still recognisably grain-shaped kernels covered in surface fungal
growth, not a wholesale replacement of the grain structure.

## Causes
Grain mold is caused by a complex of several fungal species (commonly
Fusarium, Curvularia and others) that colonise developing grain,
strongly favoured by rain and high humidity during flowering and grain
fill — the wetter that window is, the worse grain mold tends to be.
Smut is caused by specific smut fungi (Sporisorium species) that
infect the plant systemically, often from infected seed or soil, well
before the panicle emerges, and only become visible once the panicle
forms.

## Prevention
For grain mold — choose varieties with better grain mold tolerance
where available, and avoid planting so that flowering and grain fill
coincide with the peak of the regional rainy period, if sowing timing
can be adjusted. For smut — use certified, disease-free seed, since
smut is commonly seed- and soil-borne and seed source is the single
biggest prevention lever.

## Cultural Management
For grain mold — timely harvest as soon as grain reaches maturity
reduces the window fungal colonisation has to establish in wet
weather. For smut — remove and destroy infected panicles before spore
masses rupture and release, since intact bagged removal prevents
spores spreading to healthy plants and contaminating soil for future
seasons.

## Biological Management
Seed treatment with Trichoderma viride (4 g/kg seed) before sowing
suppresses both seed-borne smut inoculum and some grain mold fungal
species that also survive on seed. There is limited biological
intervention once grain mold has established on a wet, mature
panicle — prevention and cultural timing matter more here than
rescue treatment.

## Chemical Management
Smut — seed treatment with Carboxin 37.5% + Thiram 37.5% WS at 2 g/kg
seed before sowing is the primary and most effective chemical lever,
since smut infects systemically before the panicle even forms and
foliar sprays on a mature plant cannot reach or reverse that
infection. Grain mold — Propiconazole 25% EC at 1 ml/L water, PHI 20
days, re-entry 24 hours, applied at flowering and repeated once during
grain fill if wet weather continues, targeting the window when
infection actually establishes rather than after mold is already
visible.

## Monitoring
For smut — inspect emerging panicles as soon as they appear, since
infected panicles show swollen, membrane-covered structures before
rupture, and removal at that stage prevents spore release. For grain
mold — monitor weather through flowering and grain fill; a wet spell
during this window is the strongest predictor of grain mold pressure
and is worth flagging as a risk even before any visible symptom
appears.

## When To Contact Expert
Smut incidence crossing more than a few panicles per plot, since spore
spread compounds quickly once ruptured masses release; grain mold
severity threatening a large share of the harvest, particularly where
grain is intended for seed use next season, since infected seed can
carry inoculum forward.
