# Soybean Stem Borers — Stem Fly & Girdle Beetle

## Symptoms
Stem fly: small punctures on the stem near the growing tip, leading to
a zig-zag, reddish internal tunnel running down the stem; affected
plants show wilting or general weakening rather than a single obvious
break point.

Girdle beetle: larva tunnels inside the stem or branch and cuts a
distinct girdling ring around the circumference as it feeds; the stem
above the girdle dries out and commonly snaps or breaks off cleanly at
that ring, which is the single most recognisable field sign.

## Affected Parts
Main stem and branches for both. Girdle beetle damage is often visible
higher up on secondary branches as well, not just the main stem.

## Similar Pests
Both produce internal stem tunnelling and a weakened, wilting or dying
stem, so early damage can look alike from outside. The girdling ring —
a clean encircling groove cut into the stem, usually followed by a
clean snap above it — is specific to girdle beetle and is not produced
by stem fly, whose damage is a zig-zag internal tunnel without an
external ring.

## Causes
Stem fly (Melanagromyza sojae) is a small fly whose maggot mines
inside the stem after the female punctures it to lay eggs. Girdle
beetle (Nezara/Oberea, locally Obereopsis brevis) is a beetle whose
larva girdles the stem from inside as a feeding and pupation strategy,
unrelated biologically to stem fly despite the overlapping symptom.

## Prevention
Sow within the recommended window for the region — early sown crops
generally escape the worst stem fly pressure, which builds through the
season. Avoid carrying volunteer soybean plants or crop residue over
from the previous season, since both pests can carry over on stubble.

## Cultural Management
Remove and destroy stems that have already snapped from girdle beetle
damage before the larva inside completes its cycle and disperses.
Avoid excess nitrogen, which produces the soft stem tissue both pests
tunnel into more easily. Deep summer ploughing exposes overwintering
pupae to predators and heat.

## Biological Management
Conserve natural parasitoids of stem fly maggots by avoiding early,
precautionary broad-spectrum sprays. Neem seed kernel extract (5%) as
a foliar spray during early vegetative stage suppresses egg-laying for
both pests with lower impact on natural enemies than a synthetic
insecticide.

## Chemical Management
Stem fly — Thiamethoxam 25% WG at 0.2 g/L water, PHI 21 days, re-entry
24 hours, applied as a seed treatment or early foliar spray within the
first 2–3 weeks after emergence, when the maggot is still accessible
inside young tissue. Girdle beetle — Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC at
0.3 ml/L water, PHI 21 days, re-entry 24 hours, applied at first sign
of girdling before larvae move deeper into woodier stem tissue where
sprays are far less effective.

## Monitoring
Walk the field weekly from 2 weeks after emergence, checking stems for
wilting tips (stem fly) and for the distinctive girdling ring or
snapped stem ends (girdle beetle). Cut open a few wilting stems to
confirm a zig-zag internal tunnel before attributing damage to stem
fly rather than another cause.

## When To Contact Expert
Stem breakage from girdle beetle crossing roughly 10% of plants in a
patch; stem fly damage appearing widely at the seedling stage, where
losses compound fastest since the whole plant depends on that single
growing point.
