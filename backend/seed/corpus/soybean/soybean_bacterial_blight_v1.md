# Soybean Bacterial Blight (Pseudomonas savastanoi pv. glycinea)

## Symptoms
Small, water-soaked, angular leaf spots bounded by the leaf veins,
similar in shape to cotton's angular bacterial spotting; spots later
turn brown with a yellow halo; affected leaves show general yellowing
and, in heavier infections, wilting; severely spotted leaves can tear
and shred in wind and rain, giving a tattered look distinct from
fungal leaf spot diseases.

## Affected Parts
Leaves primarily, occasionally extending to pods in severe, prolonged
wet-weather outbreaks.

## Similar Diseases
The soybean leaf spot diseases (anthracnose/pod blight and alternaria
leaf spot) also produce brown leaf spotting, but both of those are
fungal and lack the angular, vein-bounded shape and water-soaked
appearance bacterial blight shows before spots dry and brown. A
tattered, torn leaf texture after wind or rain is also specific to
bacterial blight and is not produced by the fungal leaf spot diseases.

## Causes
A seed-borne and rain-splash-spread bacterium, favoured by cool, wet
weather with frequent rain — unlike many fungal soybean diseases which
favour warm humid conditions, bacterial blight tends to be worse in
cooler, wetter spells within the season.

## Prevention
Use certified, disease-free seed — this disease is commonly seed-borne
and seed source is the single biggest prevention lever. Avoid working
in or irrigating the field while foliage is wet, since bacteria spread
readily through water contact between plants. Crop rotation with a
non-host crop for at least one season reduces carryover inoculum in
field residue.

## Cultural Management
Remove and destroy infected plant debris after harvest rather than
leaving it in the field, since the bacterium survives on crop residue.
Avoid overhead irrigation during cool, wet spells when infection risk
is highest. Maintain adequate row spacing for airflow through the
canopy.

## Biological Management
Seed treatment with Pseudomonas fluorescens (talc-based formulation,
10 g/kg seed) before sowing suppresses seed-borne inoculum. Foliar
application of Bacillus subtilis-based biocontrol products during
early vegetative stage can reduce disease pressure where chemical use
is being minimised.

## Chemical Management
Copper oxychloride 50% WP at 2.5 g/L water, PHI 7 days, re-entry 24
hours, applied at first sign of angular leaf spots and repeated at
10–14 day intervals if cool, wet weather continues. There is no
curative rescue for tissue already torn or shredded — spraying limits
further spread rather than repairing existing damage.

## Monitoring
Inspect lower and mid-canopy leaves weekly, especially after a spell
of cool, wet weather — early lesions are easiest to catch on lower
leaves before they climb the plant. Distinguish angular, water-soaked
spotting from the fungal leaf spot diseases before treating, since the
chemical approach differs (copper-based bactericide vs. fungicide).

## When To Contact Expert
Rapid spread following an extended cool, wet spell, since conditions
that favour this disease can also drive it past what a single spray
round controls; any outbreak following use of home-saved, uncertified
seed, since seed-source tracing matters for preventing recurrence next
season.
