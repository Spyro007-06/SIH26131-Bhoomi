# Soybean Leaf Spot Diseases — Anthracnose/Pod Blight & Alternaria Leaf Spot

## Symptoms
Anthracnose/pod blight: irregular brown lesions on leaves, stems and
pods; small black dot-like fruiting bodies (acervuli) visible within
older lesions, often arranged in rows along leaf veins; premature
defoliation in severe infections; pods can show sunken brown lesions
that reduce seed quality.

Alternaria leaf spot: brown spots on leaves, frequently showing
concentric rings (a target-like pattern) within each spot; spots
enlarge and merge as the disease progresses; heavily spotted leaves
yellow and drop early.

## Affected Parts
Leaves for both; anthracnose additionally affects stems and pods,
which alternaria leaf spot typically does not.

## Similar Diseases
Both cause brown leaf spotting and premature leaf drop, so a photo of
scattered brown spots alone does not separate them. The discriminators:
concentric target-ring patterns within the spot point to alternaria
leaf spot; small black dot-like fruiting bodies visible in the lesion,
and any spotting extending onto stems or pods rather than staying on
leaves alone, point to anthracnose/pod blight.

## Causes
Anthracnose/pod blight is caused by Colletotrichum fungal species,
seed-borne and also spread by rain-splash within a standing crop.
Alternaria leaf spot is caused by Alternaria fungal species, favoured
by warm humid conditions and often appearing later in the season on
ageing leaf tissue, which is naturally more susceptible.

## Prevention
Use certified, disease-free seed for anthracnose, since it is commonly
seed-borne. Avoid dense plant spacing that traps humidity within the
canopy, which favours both diseases. Crop rotation with a non-host
crop for at least one season reduces carryover inoculum in field
residue for both.

## Cultural Management
Remove and destroy infected crop residue after harvest rather than
leaving it in the field, since both pathogens survive on residue.
Avoid overhead irrigation, particularly later in the season when
alternaria pressure builds on ageing leaves. Maintain adequate row
spacing for airflow through the canopy.

## Biological Management
Seed treatment with Trichoderma viride (4 g/kg seed) before sowing
suppresses seed-borne anthracnose inoculum. Foliar application of
Pseudomonas fluorescens or Bacillus subtilis-based biocontrol products
during early pod-fill stage can reduce disease pressure where chemical
use is being minimised.

## Chemical Management
Anthracnose/pod blight — Carbendazim 50% WP at 1 g/L water, PHI 21
days, re-entry 24 hours, applied at first sign of leaf or pod lesions
and repeated at 10–14 day intervals if humid weather continues.
Alternaria leaf spot — Mancozeb 75% WP at 2.5 g/L water, PHI 15 days,
re-entry 24 hours, applied once concentric-ring spotting is confirmed
and spreading. Rotate fungicide groups between sprays where more than
one application is needed within a season.

## Monitoring
Inspect lower and mid-canopy leaves weekly from flowering onward,
since both diseases typically establish there before climbing the
plant. During pod-fill, examine pods directly for early anthracnose
lesions rather than waiting for visible rot or shrivel.

## When To Contact Expert
Pod lesions from anthracnose spreading past early-detected spots into
widespread pod blight, since this directly threatens seed quality;
alternaria leaf spot causing more than 25–30% premature leaf drop
during pod-fill, when yield impact from lost leaf area is highest.
