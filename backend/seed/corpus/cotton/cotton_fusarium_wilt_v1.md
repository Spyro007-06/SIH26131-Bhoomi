# Cotton Fusarium Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. vasinfectum)

## Symptoms
Yellowing of leaves starting from the lower canopy and progressing
upward; wilting that often shows first on one side of the plant or one
branch before spreading, since the fungus blocks the vascular system
unevenly; vascular browning visible as a dark discoloration inside the
stem when cut in cross-section; gradual drying and death of the plant,
typically over days to a couple of weeks once symptoms are visible.

## Early Symptoms
Slight yellowing and drooping of lower leaves during the hottest part
of the day, recovering somewhat overnight in the earliest stage —
easy to mistake for ordinary heat stress before it becomes one-sided.

## Advanced Symptoms
One-sided or whole-plant wilting that no longer recovers overnight;
extensive leaf yellowing and drop; visible vascular browning when the
stem is cut; plant death.

## Affected Parts
The vascular (water-conducting) tissue inside the stem and root, which
is why symptoms appear as wilting and browning rather than surface
lesions — the damage is internal and plumbing-related, not a leaf spot.

## Similar Diseases
Bacterial blight also ends in blackening and plant death in severe
cases, so a photo of an already-dying plant late in either disease can
look similar. The discriminator: bacterial blight shows angular,
water-soaked leaf spots before decline; fusarium wilt shows no discrete
leaf lesions at all, only yellowing, one-sided wilting and internal
vascular browning visible on a stem cross-section.

## Causes
A soil-borne fungus that enters through the roots and colonises the
plant's vascular tissue, physically blocking water movement. It
persists in soil for years independent of a host crop, and is often
worse where root-knot nematode is also present, since nematode wounds
give the fungus an easier entry point.

## Prevention
Use resistant or tolerant varieties where available for the region —
there is no curative treatment once vascular infection is established,
so variety choice and soil management are the real prevention levers.
Avoid fields with a known history of severe wilt without a multi-year
rotation break. Manage root-knot nematode where present, since it
compounds fusarium wilt severity.

## Cultural Management
Long crop rotation (3+ years) with a non-host crop such as cereals,
since the fungus persists in soil and rotation starves it down over
time rather than eliminating it in one season. Avoid waterlogging and
correct soil drainage — wet, poorly drained soil favours root stress
that makes wilt worse. Remove and destroy wilted plants and their root
systems rather than leaving them to decompose in place, reducing local
inoculum buildup.

## Biological Management
Soil application of Trichoderma viride or Trichoderma harzianum-based
biocontrol formulations before sowing suppresses Fusarium populations
in the root zone. Seed treatment with Trichoderma viride (4 g/kg seed)
gives young roots an early competitive advantage against soil-borne
infection.

## Chemical Management
There is no curative chemical spray once vascular wilt symptoms are
visible on a standing plant — chemical intervention here is preventive
only. Carbendazim 50% WP as a seed treatment (2 g/kg seed) before
sowing reduces early seedling infection in fields with known wilt
history. There is no registered rescue treatment for an already-
wilting mature plant; management at that stage is removal of the
plant, not spraying it.

## Monitoring
Walk fields with known wilt history from early vegetative stage,
checking for one-sided wilting or drooping during peak afternoon heat
that does not recover by early morning. Cut a suspect stem near the
base to check for the characteristic dark vascular browning before
confirming a case, since surface wilting alone overlaps with drought
stress and other root problems.

## When To Contact Expert
Wilt appearing in a field with no prior history, since this can signal
a fresh soil contamination worth tracing; wilt spreading rapidly across
a patch rather than isolated single plants, which can indicate a
severe soil inoculum load or compounding nematode pressure needing
laboratory confirmation.
